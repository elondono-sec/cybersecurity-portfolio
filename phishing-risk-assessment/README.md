# Phishing Risk Assessment & Security Awareness

**Project Type:** Academic Cybersecurity Risk Assessment and Security Awareness Training  
**Focus:** Phishing Detection | Social Engineering | Risk Assessment | Security Awareness

## Project Overview

This project examines phishing as a cybersecurity threat and explores how organizations can reduce the likelihood and impact of successful social engineering attacks.

The project draws on academic cybersecurity risk assessment work involving a fictional cloud-storage phishing scenario and security awareness training concepts practiced through the Mastercard Cybersecurity Job Simulation.

The emphasis is on identifying phishing risks, evaluating potential organizational impact, and recommending preventive and detective security controls.

## Project Objectives

- Identify common phishing and social engineering techniques.
- Evaluate the security risks associated with deceptive emails.
- Understand how attackers exploit trust, urgency, and impersonation.
- Assess potential impacts on organizational data and accounts.
- Recommend technical and administrative security controls.
- Explore security awareness training and employee reporting practices.

## Phishing Risk Scenario

The academic case involved a fictional phishing scenario associated with a cloud-storage service.

Such attacks may attempt to convince users to open malicious links, enter credentials into fraudulent websites, or disclose sensitive information.

The scenario was used to evaluate phishing-related security risks and appropriate defensive responses.

## Threat Analysis

| Threat Element | Security Concern |
|---|---|
| Sender impersonation | Users may trust messages that appear to come from legitimate services |
| Malicious links | Users may be redirected to fraudulent login pages |
| Credential harvesting | Attackers may attempt to obtain account credentials |
| Urgent messaging | Time pressure can encourage unsafe decisions |
| Account compromise | Stolen credentials may enable unauthorized access |
| Data exposure | Compromised accounts may expose organizational information |

## Risk Assessment

Phishing risks can be evaluated by considering the likelihood of successful deception and the potential consequences of compromised accounts or information.

### Potential Business Impacts

- Unauthorized account access
- Exposure of sensitive organizational information
- Business email compromise
- Financial fraud
- Operational disruption
- Incident response and recovery costs
- Reputational damage

Risk severity depends on the affected systems, user privileges, available security controls, and the information accessible through compromised accounts.

## Security Awareness Training

The Mastercard Cybersecurity Job Simulation provided experience with phishing awareness training concepts, including designing a phishing email simulation and interpreting simulated campaign results.

This reinforced the importance of helping employees recognize suspicious messages and respond appropriately.

### Training Topics

1. Verify unexpected requests for credentials or sensitive information.
2. Inspect sender information and links carefully.
3. Recognize urgency, impersonation, and unusual requests.
4. Avoid entering credentials through unverified links.
5. Report suspicious messages using approved organizational channels.
6. Follow incident reporting procedures after suspected exposure.

## Recommended Security Controls

| Control | Security Benefit |
|---|---|
| Multifactor authentication | Adds protection against password-based account compromise |
| Email filtering | Helps identify and block suspicious messages |
| SPF, DKIM, and DMARC | Support email authentication and domain spoofing defenses |
| Security awareness training | Helps employees recognize social engineering attempts |
| Phishing reporting process | Enables faster investigation and response |
| Least-privilege access | Limits the potential impact of compromised accounts |
| Centralized logging | Supports investigation of suspicious authentication activity |
| Incident response procedures | Improves containment and recovery |

## Security Awareness Program Recommendations

- Provide recurring, role-appropriate phishing awareness education.
- Conduct authorized phishing simulations where organizational policy permits.
- Offer constructive feedback rather than punitive responses.
- Make suspicious email reporting straightforward.
- Track aggregate reporting and training outcomes over time.
- Review and update training materials as phishing techniques evolve.

## Evidence and Scope

This report summarizes academic cybersecurity work and a completed cybersecurity job simulation.

The cloud-storage scenario was fictional. The Mastercard exercise was a simulated training experience, not an independently operated phishing campaign against real employees.

No real organizational credentials were collected, and no independently verified phishing campaign results are presented in this repository.

## Conclusion

This project demonstrates how phishing risk assessment, technical security controls, employee awareness, and incident reporting can work together to reduce exposure to social engineering attacks.

The experience supports skills relevant to security operations, governance, risk and compliance (GRC), and cybersecurity awareness programs.
